## Positioning

Maker Compass is the step before the build: it turns a rough idea into the market context, product plan, first-version scope, mockups, and technical direction a serious builder needs before writing code.

The wedge is judgment, not generation. Every other tool in this category competes on how fast it can produce software; Maker Compass competes on whether the software was worth producing. AI made software easier to generate, not easier to make meaningful, and the gap between "I have an idea" and "I know what to build first" is where most ideas quietly die. The brand's job is to make slowing down at the start feel like the professional move it is.

That means the product's own surfaces have to demonstrate the argument. A tool that sells direction cannot ship a hype-styled interface. Restraint here is a proof, not a taste preference.

## Audience

**Primary segment.** Founders and indie builders carrying one specific idea they have not turned into a real first version. They can already generate code. What they cannot do is decide which version of the idea deserves the next three months.

**Secondary segments.** Product-minded operators inside companies who need to pressure-test an idea before asking for headcount, and technical consultants who scope other people's ideas for a living and want the reasoning legible to a client.

**Anchor persona.** Priya, 31, a senior engineer with a side project she has restarted four times. Each restart began with a different framework and ended in the same place: a repo with authentication, a settings page, and no answer to who it is for. She does not need help writing code and finds "AI builds your app" pitches insulting. She wants someone competent to help her cut the idea down to something she can put in front of ten people in three weeks.

**The trigger.** Starting feels messy. Tools, prompts, feature possibilities, and pressure to ship have created motion without direction.

## Mission

Help builders choose an intentional first version before spending weeks or asking AI to build the wrong thing.

---

## Values

1. **Direction over momentum.** Random features create motion, not progress.
2. **Judgment over generation.** Generation is cheap now. Deciding what to generate is not.
3. **Respect the idea.** A messy idea is meaningful. Treat it like it belongs to someone's life.
4. **Show the work.** Name the actual output and the next decision. Never sell a feeling.
5. **Earn the restraint.** Every word and pixel has to justify itself, including ours.

## Story — Why We Exist

Most abandoned projects were never bad ideas. They were unscoped ones. Someone had a real problem in view, opened an editor, and started with the parts that were easy to start with: the login screen, the settings page, the framework decision. Months later the repo has everything except an answer to who it is for.

Generation got cheap. That did not make building easier, it made the wrong build faster. You can now produce a plausible application in a weekend without ever deciding what the application is for, and the tooling will cheerfully help you do it. The bottleneck moved from typing to thinking, and almost nothing moved with it.

Maker Compass is the step people skip. Give it the rough idea and it gives back the market context around it, an honest read on the first version worth building, the scope to hold that version to, mockups to react to, and the technical direction to start from. Not a finished product. A direction you can defend, before you spend your life on the wrong one.

## Voice & Tone

**Adjectives:** quietly direct, anti-hype, specific, constructively critical

**How we sound.** Like a builder who has watched too many vague projects die in a repository. Short sentences, plain verbs, no warm-up. We say "first version" more often than "MVP", and we never promise instant startups, magic apps, or effortless success.

**Copy examples by context**

**Auxiliary, product-internal only.** Warm Coral `#F4A261`, Warm Sand `#E9C46A`, Warm Ember `#D95F3B` communicate status, dependency, and advisory states inside the product. They never appear in marketing, on the landing, or in the mark.

**Named rules.**

- **The Tinted Neutral Rule.** Pure `#000` and pure `#FFF` are forbidden as page grounds. Every neutral carries a warm tilt.
- **Contrast.** Workshop Black on Cloud is 15.6:1. Action Red on Cloud is 4.8:1, which passes AA for body text but not for text under 18px on Card White; use Workshop Black for small text and reserve red for fills and large type.

## Typography

**Display and body:** Hanken Grotesk. **Label and metadata:** Fira Mono.

One committed sans carries the whole system from hero display at weight 800 down to body at 400; the range is wide enough to build hierarchy inside one family. Fira Mono handles kicker labels with quiet technical authority. The pairing lands in the tech-minimal lane without the over-familiarity of Inter or IBM Plex.

| Role | Font | Weight | Size | Line height | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Hanken Grotesk | 800 | `clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 4.5rem)` | 0.95 | -0.05em |
| Headline | Hanken Grotesk | 800 | `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.35rem)` | 0.98 | -0.05em |
| Title | Hanken Grotesk | 700 | 1.5rem | 1.2 | -0.02em |
| Body | Hanken Grotesk | 400 | 1rem | 1.6 | normal |
| Label | Fira Mono | 500 | 0.6875rem UPPERCASE | 1.2 | 0.18em |

**Named rules.**

- **The Tight Tracking Rule.** Display and Headline tracking is `-0.05em`, never flat zero. Large type at default tracking reads as a wireframe.
- **The Mono-Label Rule.** Metadata, status, and kicker labels are Fira Mono UPPERCASE at `0.18em`. Not Hanken small caps, not Hanken at a lighter weight. The mono shift is how the system says "this is metadata, not voice."
- **Reading measure.** Cap long-form output at 65 to 75 characters per line. Body line-height 1.6 is deliberately generous; this is a tool people read in for minutes.

Fonts ship in `fonts/` as OFL-licensed files: `HankenGrotesk[wght].ttf` (variable, all weights), `HankenGrotesk-Italic[wght].ttf`, and Fira Mono Regular / Medium / Bold.

**Non-Latin fallback.** Every font stack must carry broad-script fallbacks so a non-Latin name never renders as tofu: display `"Hanken Grotesk", "Noto Sans SC", "Noto Sans TC", "Noto Sans JP", "Noto Sans KR", "Noto Sans Arabic", system-ui, sans-serif`.

## Logo

### Symbol — the bearing wedge

A single solid wedge: a compass needle rotated 32° off vertical, with a notch cut into its tail. It is a bearing, not a compass rose. The off-axis rotation is the whole idea: a decided direction rather than a centred, neutral, still-deciding one. No circle, no cardinal points, no ornament.

The notch reads from 64px up. At 32px and below the notch collapses into noise, so those exports use a simplified solid silhouette with the same outline and optical weight. The switch is automatic in the build script and invisible in use.

Geometry is authored, not traced: `logo/symbol/geometry.json` holds the exact coordinates on a 1024 grid, and `build-symbol.py` regenerates every export from it.

### Wordmark

`MakerCompass`, set solid as one word in Hanken Grotesk with tracking pulled to `-0.045em`. The ownable move is structural: **`Maker` at weight 800 against `Compass` at weight 500**. The weight break carries the hierarchy the brand argues for, direction first and subject second, and it survives being flattened to a single colour, which a two-tone wordmark would not.

Glyphs are converted to real outlines, so the SVG has no font dependency and no hinting surprises.

### Lockups

Cap height is the unit of measure, not the wordmark bounding box (which also carries the descender and padding).

- **Horizontal:** symbol at 1.3 cap height, gap of 0.5 cap. The symbol is optically centred on the cap band, not the box, so the `p` descender does not drag it below the visual midline.
- **Stacked:** symbol at 1.9 cap height, gap of 0.45 cap, wordmark centred beneath.
- **Clear space:** one cap height on all four sides. Nothing enters it.
- **Minimum sizes:** wordmark 90px wide on screen. Symbol 16px. Horizontal lockup 140px wide; below that, drop to the symbol alone.

### Colour variants

Four per asset: `red` (Action Red on light), `black` (Workshop Black on light), `cream` (Warm Paper on dark), `white` (on photography and on Action Red fills).

### Files

```
logo/symbol/    symbol-{red,black,cream,white}.svg        vector master
                symbol-red-small.svg                      simplified small-size cut
                symbol-{colour}-{16..2048}.png            8 sizes x 4 colours, true alpha
                geometry.json                             authored coordinates
logo/wordmark/  wordmark-{colour}.svg                     outlined paths, no font needed
                wordmark-{colour}.png                     1024px wide, transparent
logo/lockup/horizontal/ lockup-h-{colour}.{svg,png}
logo/lockup/stacked/    lockup-s-{colour}.{svg,png}
icons/                  12 UI icons as standalone SVGs, currentColor
```

### Don'ts

Never stretch or condense. Never rotate the wordmark or re-rotate the symbol (32° is fixed geometry, not a suggestion). Never recolour outside the four variants. Never add a drop shadow, outline, or gradient. Never place the mark on a busy area of a photograph; use the white variant over a dark, quiet region. Never rebuild the lockup by eye; regenerate it.

## Icons

Digital product, so the icon system matters.

**Construction.** 24×24 viewBox. 1.5px stroke. `stroke="currentColor"`, so icons inherit the surrounding text colour. Butt caps and miter joins for square-cut terminals. No fills. Geometry snapped to a 2px grid wherever the form allows.

**Curves are rationed.** Only the search lens and the info disc are round, because those forms genuinely are. Everything else stays angular so the set echoes the wedge rather than fighting it.

**Colour.** Icons never carry Action Red. Red is commitment; an icon is navigation.

**The set** (12, in `icons/`): `arrow-right`, `check`, `close`, `plus`, `menu`, `search`, `user`, `settings`, `bell`, `info`, plus two brand-specific glyphs, `scope` (the bracketed first-version boundary) and `build-map` (one trajectory, three milestones).

**Beyond the set.** Lucide matches the stroke weight and terminal style most closely. Re-cap any borrowed icon to butt terminals before use.

**Adding one.** Start from the nearest existing glyph rather than a blank grid, keep coordinates on the 2px grid, and check it at 18px before shipping. Three icons were redrawn in this edition because their detail closed into a blob at that size: `settings`, `bell`, and `build-map`.

## Imagery — Photography

Photography-led. No illustration in the visual world; where a diagram is needed, the product's own generated artifacts (build maps, scope tables, mockups) do that job, which is also the honest thing to show.

- **Subject.** Evidence of thinking in progress: paper with hand-drawn wireframe boxes and arrows, index cards in columns, pinned cards on a wall, kraft folders, a wooden ruler. Closed laptops as often as open ones.
- **Light.** Real light only. Window light, overcast daylight, low late-afternoon rake. Never studio strobes.
- **Colour.** Warm neutrals (warm paper, oatmeal, wood, off-white, warm grey, warm black) with exactly one red object in frame and nothing else red.
- **Cast.** Racially diverse across any set featuring people. Name a specific ethnicity per prompt rather than trusting the model default, which skews white. Vary body types and ages, not only skin tone. Subjects are working, not addressing the camera.
- **Texture.** Visible film grain always. Rooms that have been used. Slight imperfections.
- **Screens in frame.** Grey placeholder rectangles and at most one red rectangle. No readable interface text; brand text is composited later so it stays editable and correctly typeset.
- **Forbidden.** Purple or blue gradients, glassmorphism, sparkles, glowing effects, robots, holograms, floating UI, neon, dark command-center rooms, coworking-space aesthetics, stock-photo energy, perfect symmetry, AI-smooth surfaces, objects floating on pure white, people smiling at the camera.
- **Banned cliché concepts.** Lightbulbs, hourglasses, handshakes, network nodes, glowing brains, rockets, ladders, mazes, puzzle pieces, upward glowing arrows, humanoid AI figures, and a literal compass held up to the lens.

Full prompt template in `prompts/photography.md`.

## Visual World

A lit workshop, not a dark command center. Morning or late-afternoon light across a wooden table. Paper is the medium of thought: sheets marked in black ink, cards sorted into columns, one card half-pinned to a plaster wall. Somebody is mid-decision, or has just stepped away from one. The single red object in frame is where the commitment is.

The world is deliberately pre-digital, because the argument is that the thinking happens before the tooling. Four reference frames ship in `images/`.

## Touchpoints

- **App icon / social avatar.** Symbol only, white on Action Red, no wordmark. Sharp corners on the asset; the platform applies its own rounding.
- **Favicon.** `symbol-red-32.png` and `symbol-red-16.png`, the simplified silhouette. Verify at true size, never scaled down in a browser.
- **Web header.** Horizontal lockup, Workshop Black, 140px minimum.
- **Product surfaces.** Symbol alone in the navigation rail. Rounded 8px containers inside `/(dashboard)`, sharp corners on landing surfaces.
- **Laptop and desk context.** Screens show real product artifacts, never invented UI.
- **Print collateral.** Stacked lockup, Workshop Black on uncoated warm stock. Get Action Red matched physically before printing.

## Do & Don't

**Do**

1. Do leave one full cap height of clear space around the lockup, including in social crops.
2. Do let one element dominate every composition. Flat hierarchy is the tell of an undesigned page.
3. Do name the artifact ("your first-version scope", "the competitive read") instead of the feature.
4. Do use Fira Mono UPPERCASE at `0.18em` for every kicker and metadata line, and nothing else.
5. Do show the product's real output, including when it is long and unglamorous. Long-form artifacts get the same craft as the marketing copy.

**Don't**

1. Don't put Action Red on more than 10% of a screen, and never on two unrelated elements in one composition.
2. Don't use red for errors or focus rings. Errors are Destructive `#C0392B`; focus rings are Slate Plum `#4A4040`.
3. Don't set display type at flat tracking. `-0.05em` or it reads as a wireframe.
4. Don't imply the product writes the app for you. It decides what the app should be.
5. Don't ship a gradient hero, a glass panel, a sparkle, or a "Powered by AI" badge. The brand's argument dies with any one of them.

## How to Use This Spec

- **With an AI tool.** Paste `prompts/system-prompt.md`, then your task. For imagery, paste `prompts/photography.md` instead.
- **With a developer.** Hand over `tokens/` and `logo/`. Point at `build-tokens.py` as the regeneration path.
- **With a designer.** Hand over this file plus `quick-brand.pdf`. The colour, type, and lockup measurements here are the spec; the PDF is the argument for them.
- **Picking a logo file.** Favicon → `symbol-red-32.png`. App icon → symbol white on Action Red. Web header → `lockup-h-black.svg`. Dark background → `-cream` or `-white`. Over photography → `-white`.
- **Regenerating assets.** Run in order: `build-symbol.py`, `build-wordmark.py`, `build-icons.py`,
  `build-tokens.py`, `build-composites.py`, then `build-pdf.py` and `build-book.py`. Later scripts
  read what earlier ones write. Requires the fonts in `fonts/`, Chrome for rasterisation, and
  numpy plus Pillow for the touchpoint compositing.
