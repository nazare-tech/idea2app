# Maker Compass Brand Foundation

Status: active foundation
Source detail: `marketing/brand-message.md`

## Name And One-Line Description

- Name: Maker Compass
- Description: Maker Compass turns a rough software idea into the market context, product plan, first-version scope, mockups, technical direction, and build-ready next step a serious builder needs.

## Audience

- Primary audience: Founders, indie builders, and product-minded operators carrying an idea they have not turned into a real first version.
- Situation or trigger: Starting feels messy; tools, prompts, feature possibilities, and pressure to ship have created motion without direction.

## Problem, Promise, And Point Of View

- Problem: The gap between “I have an idea” and “I know what to build first” is vague, noisy, and easy to avoid.
- Promise: Help the builder choose an intentional first version before spending weeks or asking AI to build the wrong thing.
- Differentiated point of view: AI makes software easier to generate, not easier to make meaningful. Direction beats speed without product signal.
- Primary line: Stop putting your life's work off. Start with direction.

## Personality And Voice

- Quietly direct: Speak like a builder who has seen too many vague projects die in a repository.
- Anti-hype: Never promise instant startups, magic apps, or effortless success.
- Respectful of the idea: Treat a messy idea as meaningful.
- Specific about value: Name the actual outputs and the next decision.
- Constructively critical: Call out shallow building without becoming cynical.
- Copy style: Short sentences, plain verbs, and “first version” more often than “MVP.”
- Avoid: unlock, supercharge, revolutionize, 10x, powered by AI, vague founder-journey language, and constant use of “AI slop.”

## Messaging Pillars

1. **The start is the hard part.** Builders need a bridge from idea to a clear first build.
2. **Faster is not always clearer.** Generation should follow product judgment.
3. **The first version should be intentional.** It is the smallest serious proof that the idea deserves more time.
4. **Direction beats momentum theater.** Random features create motion, not progress.

## Proof Needed

- [ ] Examples showing a rough idea becoming a sharper first-version plan
- [ ] Customer language describing reduced confusion or avoided rework
- [ ] Evidence that generated deliverables lead to a concrete build/test decision

## Visual Direction

- Desired territory: A lit workshop, not a dark command center.
- System: Warm neutral background, black text, one red action accent, strong typography, sharp layout, and visible planning artifacts.
- Show: Plans, choices, scope, mockups, and build steps.
- Avoid: Purple-blue gradients, glass panels, sparkles, generic robot imagery, and stock SaaS icon grids.

## Calls To Action

- Primary: Start with your idea; Find the first version; Build with direction; Turn an idea into a plan.
- Secondary: See how it works; Read the example; Review the process; Compare the paths.

## Decisions And Open Questions

- Confirmed: Direction over hype; first-version language; anti-slop positioning used sparingly; warm workshop visual territory.
- Assumptions: The primary audience values clarity enough to slow the beginning down.
- Open questions: Which proof points and customer stories make the positioning most credible?
